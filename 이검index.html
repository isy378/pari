A NOTE ON THE EXPERIENCE

THIS IS NOT
A FILTER.

A filter changes an image. Perception changes what information matters in the first place.

BIRD Wide field; roughly 2.5–8× human visual acuity; rapid lens and membrane focus; ultraviolet cues. Some species use Earth’s magnetic field for orientation. BEE 5,000–9,000 ommatidia per compound eye, 300°+ panorama and three light-sensitive ocelli; sees ultraviolet, not red; reads polarized light and flicker up to about 300 Hz. SNAKE Near-focused, low-detail sight with limited depth; shifts its lens to focus. A clear spectacle protects the eye. Blue-green and UV cues combine with pit-organ heat signals. DOG Blue-yellow color emphasis; red-green differences are muted. About 0.1–0.3 human acuity, a 200–290° field and strong sensitivity to motion. CAT Rod-rich eyes and a reflective layer help in about one-sixth the light humans need; fewer cones, reduced red-green distinction and around 200° of view.
FLY / RESEARCH NOTES

Drosophila has seven known rhodopsins, but they are not seven color channels packed into each facet. In a typical ommatidium, R1–R6 express Rh1; R7/R8 pairings express Rh3/Rh5 or Rh4/Rh6. Approximate spectral peaks shown here are Rh3 345 nm, Rh4 375 nm, Rh5 437 nm, Rh1 478 nm and Rh6 508 nm. Rh2 is associated with the simple ocelli. Rh7 has extra-retinal light-sensing roles, including circadian regulation. Red sensitivity is weaker, not a simple inability to see red.
